Why the cost is flat in width

Finding: the lateral boundary conditions are px(1) = −px(0) (mirror) but vx(1) = vx(0) (periodic). For a left/right-mirrored gait the lateral velocity must also flip sign, vx(1) = −vx(0). With the current pair, the COM simply drifts laterally at constant speed (vx ≈ −(w/2)/t_f, see the vx(0) column) and the sign reversal needed at the next step happens for free in the coordinate flip — an uncosted impulsive redirection. The lateral work ∫|Fx·vx| is only the tiny amount needed to keep the drift going (0.004 at width 0.30 vs 0.36 vertical), so widening the stance costs almost nothing. This is exactly the step-to-step-transition cost that makes wide steps expensive in humans (Donelan/Kuo), and it is currently absent.

Check: re-running with vx(1) = −vx(0) (scratch copy, same c_fr2) gives width 0 unchanged (work 0.411), but widths 0.1/0.2 hit the iteration limit at work ≈ 42 and 0.3 is infeasible — the same blow-up documented in the 2026-07-05 log for the pz sweep. So the correct BC makes lateral redirection very expensive with push-only legs at this fixed cadence; getting a clean U-shaped curve needs that BC plus the cadence/knee-cost fixes from the log's next-steps list.
